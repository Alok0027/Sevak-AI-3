## Diagnostic threshold for gestational diabetes

A single-step test is used: 75 g oral glucose, with blood sugar measured at
2 hours. "Threshold blood sugar level of >=140 mg/dL (more than or equal to
140) is taken as cut off for diagnosis of GDM."

The test does not require the woman to be fasting, which is what makes it
usable at a community contact rather than only at a facility.

## When testing happens in pregnancy

First test at the initial antenatal contact, as early in pregnancy as
possible. If negative, a second test at 24-28 weeks. At least four weeks
between the two tests. A woman first presenting after 28 weeks is tested
once, at that contact.

## First-line management: medical nutrition therapy

"All Pregnant women who test positive for GDM for the first time should be
started on Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT) and physical exercise for 2
weeks. The woman should walk/exercise for 30 mins a day."

Medical nutrition therapy means carbohydrate-controlled balanced meals with
calorie intake calculated for her BMI, activity level and trimester.

## When drug treatment starts

After two weeks of medical nutrition therapy, post-prandial blood sugar
decides the next step.

- Post-prandial below 120 mg/dL: continue nutrition therapy, review monthly
- Post-prandial 120 mg/dL or above: begin drug treatment

Metformin may be used after 20 weeks of gestation, to a maximum of 2 g
daily (500 mg twice daily). Insulin may be started at any point in
pregnancy, and is used where metformin is insufficient, or before 20 weeks
where nutrition therapy has failed.

## Referral criteria in gestational diabetes

Refer to a higher facility for:

- nausea or vomiting preventing oral intake
- fasting blood sugar above 200 mg/dL despite therapy
- post-prandial above 250 mg/dL despite medical management
- post-prandial above 200 mg/dL at any point
- total daily insulin above 20 units
- more than one hypoglycaemic episode a day
- refusal of insulin therapy
