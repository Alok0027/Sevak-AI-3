## Haemoglobin testing schedule in pregnancy

Haemoglobin is tested at four points in pregnancy: at registration (with a
complete blood count), at 20-24 weeks, at 28-32 weeks, and at 36 weeks. The
complete blood count is ideally done at the first visit.

Repeat the test if the most recent haemoglobin value is more than one month
old, or when clinical signs suggest anaemia regardless of the last value.

## Iron, folic acid and deworming regimen

Pregnant women who are not anaemic receive prophylaxis: one tablet of IFA
containing 60 mg elemental iron plus 500 micrograms folic acid, continuing
for 180 tablets after delivery.

For mild anaemia the dose doubles, to IFA 60 mg twice daily.

Deworming is a single tablet of albendazole 400 mg, given once after 12
weeks of gestation.

## Referral criteria for anaemia

Refer to a facility for:

- moderate or severe anaemia requiring detailed workup
- haemodynamic instability
- poor response to oral iron therapy
- severe anaemia, haemoglobin below 7 g/dL, requiring parenteral iron or
  transfusion

The instruction to the community health worker is prompt referral in case
of severe or moderate anaemia, or any complication.
