## Haemoglobin thresholds defining anaemia in pregnancy

For pregnant women the diagnostic cut-offs are:

- No anaemia: haemoglobin 11 g/dL or above
- Mild anaemia: 10 to 10.9 g/dL
- Moderate anaemia: 7 to 9.9 g/dL
- Severe anaemia: below 7 g/dL

Anaemia in pregnancy is graded on these bands rather than on symptoms,
because a woman can be moderately anaemic and report feeling well.

## Iron and folic acid dosing by severity of anaemia

Mild anaemia: two IFA tablets per day, one in the morning and one in the
evening, for at least 100 days.

Moderate anaemia: the same oral regimen, with investigation for an
underlying cause before treatment is started.

Severe anaemia in the second or third trimester: injectable intramuscular
iron is preferred over oral supplementation -- "Iron dextran or iron
sorbitol citrate complex given as 100 mg (2 ml) deep IM".

Haemoglobin below 7 g/dL: immediate hospitalisation, with blood
transfusion considered.

## When anaemia requires referral to a facility

Refer a pregnant woman when haemoglobin falls below 8 g/dL.

Refer also where there is poor compliance with oral iron, or an inadequate
response after one month of oral iron therapy -- a haemoglobin that has not
risen on treatment is itself a reason to escalate, not a reason to continue
the same treatment.
