## Maternal danger signs after delivery

Signs during the postnatal period that require referral:

- "Excessive bleeding, i.e. soaking more than 2-3 pads in 20-30 minutes
  after delivery"
- "Yellowness of urine, skin or eyes"
- "Pale skin or eyes, Giddiness or weakness"
- "Cracked and painful nipples"
- "Convulsions"
- "Fever"
- "Easy fatiguability/not feeling well"
- "Swelling on face, hands and legs"
- severe abdominal pain
- "Difficulty in breathing"
- "Foul-smelling lochia"
- retention of urine
- "Abnormal behaviour"
- signs of urinary tract infection
- urinary incontinence
- faecal incontinence

Swelling of the face and hands after delivery belongs on this list for the
same reason it does in pregnancy: with a raised blood pressure it points at
pre-eclampsia, which does not end at delivery.

## Action on finding a postnatal danger sign

Where a danger sign is found, the ASHA arranges transport for the woman to
the nearest health facility, using the emergency ambulance service.

The instruction is to arrange transport rather than to advise attendance:
the distinction matters, because a referral a woman cannot physically reach
is not a referral.
